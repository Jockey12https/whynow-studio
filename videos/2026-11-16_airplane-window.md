# 2026-11-16 - Why airplane windows have a tiny hole (evergreen)

YouTube title: Why Airplane Windows Have a Tiny Hole #Shorts

YouTube description:
That tiny hole near the bottom of a plane window is a "bleed" (breather) hole in the middle pane. It lets cabin air equalize in the gap, so the outer pane carries the pressure load and the middle pane stays in reserve as a backup. It also lets moisture out, which keeps the window from fogging.

Fact sheet (checked against Fortune, AFAR, Gizmodo, Mental Floss, BGR, YourMileageMayVary):
- Windows are three acrylic panes (outer, middle, inner "scratch" pane). Checked.
- The tiny hole is in the middle pane, near the bottom ("bleed" or "breather" hole). Checked.
- It equalizes cabin pressure with the gap between outer and middle panes, so the outer pane carries the cabin pressure load. Checked.
- The middle pane is the fail-safe if the outer pane fails ("extremely rare"). Checked.
- The hole also lets moisture escape and reduces fog or condensation. Checked.
Could not verify / avoided: exact pressure numbers, any specific aircraft model figures. Some sources swap the names of the middle and inner panes; the video follows Gizmodo, AFAR and Fortune (hole in the MIDDLE pane).
